## What is inside

    music/<track>/<track>_intro    5 to 10 s, fades in from silence and ends exactly
                                   where the loop begins
    music/<track>/<track>_loop     the main part, 1 to 2 minutes, loops without a gap
    music/<track>/<track>_outro    starts exactly where the loop ends and fades out
    music/jingles/victory          a short victory jingle (no loop)
    music/<track>/info.txt         mood, tempo, instruments and suggested use of the track
    loops.json                     loop start and end of every loop, in samples and seconds,
                                   plus the track attributes

48 kHz stereo, WAV 16 bit and OGG Vorbis. All loops sit at the same loudness
(-18 dBFS RMS), so you can switch tracks without touching the volume.

## Track guide

Tempo is given as chord changes per minute: how often the harmony moves says more
about how calm a track feels than a beat count. Under 6 per minute means long held
chords, above 16 the harmony moves every few seconds. Tracks driven by drums say
"driving beat" instead. Brightness describes the
sound colour (dark, warm, bright), mode is minor, major, modal or unclear when the
track keeps its key open. The same attributes are in loops.json and in an info.txt
next to every track.

## How the intro, loop and outro work

1. Entering an area: play <track>_intro once, then <track>_loop on repeat.
   The intro ends on the exact sample the loop starts with, so there is no bump.
2. Leaving the area: when the loop reaches its end, play <track>_outro instead of
   the next repeat. It continues seamlessly and fades out. In a hurry? Crossfade
   from wherever the loop is into the outro over about a second.
3. Start the next area's intro while the outro fades.

The loop files need no extra fade and no crossfade. If your engine wants loop
points, loops.json has them (loop_start_sample is 0, loop_end_sample is the file
length). Godot 4: in the import dock of an OGG set Loop to on. Unity: tick Loop on
the AudioSource.
